# OOO

A first-person "metroidbrainia": escape an impossibly vast, empty midcentury-modern brutalist office. Progress is gated only by knowledge, so an expert walks out in about two minutes. Nothing is ever explained. There is no HUD, no tutorial, no dialogue, no fail state and no timer. The name reads as both "Out Of Office" and "000", and that double reading is the last secret.

## Direction (three sketches, one pick)

The brief asks for one or two impossible rules explored deeply. I sketched three:

- **A. Same number, same place (picked).** Any opening that carries a number plate is a *threshold*. Two thresholds showing the same number are one threshold: go in through one and you come out of the other, keeping your speed. Plates are office letterboard (movable tiles), so changing a number rewires the building. Progression comes from learning what counts as a number, what counts as an opening, and where the one exterior number is.
- **B. The floor plan is true.** The evacuation maps on the walls describe the building exactly. Rotating or editing a map rearranges the rooms. Rejected: this is the shifting-room conceit the brief rules out (Control), and the map-editing interface drifts toward explaining.
- **C. Every floor is the same floor.** The office repeats endlessly. A change on one floor shows up on every copy, and you progress by finding the floor that differs, then leave by going below zero. Rejected: repeated floors work against five distinct areas and a Riven-like sense of place, the movement vantages mean less in identical copies, and the puzzles collapse into spot-the-difference.

**Why A:**
- Offices are covered in numbers (rooms, aisles, floors, desks, menus). The brief's diegetic text therefore becomes the puzzle language, and nothing has to speak to the player.
- One rule goes deep through five discoveries (K1-K5 below).
- It works with the momentum movement: you carry speed through a threshold, and a fall becomes a launch.
- An expert's exit depends only on knowledge.
- It shares mechanics with portal games but differs in kind. There is no portal gun: every link is fixed office signage that the player rewrites by hand.

**Private backstory (never stated):** the player is an employee who never clocked out. The building kept numbering itself to keep them inside, and there is no room 000 because zero means *not here*. This only guides set dressing: names on nameplates, stale memos, one desk that is still warm.

## 60-second pitch

The player opens their eyes in a wood-panelled waiting room. Rows of curved orange plastic chairs face a shuttered reception hatch with a NOW SERVING 104 display, philodendrons stand in concrete planters, and fog hangs in the light. Door 104 leads into a long concrete corridor. They sprint, slide under a half-lowered security shutter, and reach a second door at the end, also marked 104. Through it they are back in the waiting room, entering by its own door, still moving. In the corridor, a transom window above a side door sits just within a wall run. They take it and burst out onto a gallery four storeys above a vast foggy brutalist atrium, with light falling in shafts from the skylights. No words have appeared on screen.

## Core loop

1. Move through a space that invites a move (a wall to run, a ramp to surf, a gap to clear).
2. Notice something off: a number repeats, a door leads somewhere it can't.
3. Form a theory about the rule, then test it by crossing, carrying or rearranging tiles.
4. The theory opens a new area, shortcut or vantage point that shows more of the building.
5. Repeat until you understand enough to make the number that leads out.

Feel: **flowing** (fast, playful movement in a slow, melancholic space).

## The rule and the knowledge graph

**Threshold:** a door frame, arch, window, floor hatch, aisle sign or opening that carries a number plate. A threshold **pairs** when exactly one other threshold in the world shows the same number. Paired thresholds render the far side and link the player through. If no other threshold has the number, or three or more do, the threshold is an ordinary opening and shows what is really behind it. There are two plate types: screwed brass plates are fixed, and felt letterboard plates take movable tiles.

| Knowledge | Learned in | What it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K1: matching numbers connect | A1 (the 104 loop) | understanding the loops; door 1 from the waiting room to the lobby |
| K2: tiles move, and moving them rewires | A3 (the 37 records passage) | breaking loops; building your own links |
| K3: floors and ceilings count; speed is turned, not lost | A5 (shaft hatch 6) | the launch to A6 and the high vantage |
| K4: the building can't tell letter O from 0 | A4 (menu board "S0UP", "C0FFEE"), A3 (Facilities memo) | an unlimited supply of zeros |
| K5: one number exists outside: 000 | A6 (boardroom window: 000 on the plain; whiteboard "000 — ?") | the way out |

**Expert route:** from spawn, walk through reception door 1, which pairs with the lobby's front door 1. Wall-run along the lobby's south wall and pull three O tiles from "OUT OF OFFICE" on the directory. The cafeteria menu also has letter O's ("TODAY", "TOAST") at floor height. Set the tiles on the cloakroom's blank letterboard plate so it reads 000, and step out into the fog. About two minutes. The play-mode test `ExpertRoute_DoorOne_LetterOs_Cloakroom_Out` runs it end to end.

## Areas (six, connected around the Atrium hub)

Floors are 4 m apart. Each area gets its own builder or prefab so areas can be built in parallel.

- **A1 Waiting Room (L4, spawn):**
  - inside_02 chairs, control_01 wood, the NOW SERVING display. Reception door 1 leads to the lobby.
  - The corridor's two ends are the 104 pair, facing each other, so it loops endlessly.
  - Exits: a high transom window (a hop from the radiator bench, or a wall run) or a slide under a half-lowered shutter (1.25 m gap), both onto the Atrium L4 gallery.
- **A2 Atrium (L0-L6, hub):** about 40 x 60 m with a 26 m void, galleries on every level, a monumental stair L0-L2, a central planter with a tree, skylight shafts, and severance_01 seating pits at L0. The lobby's revolving doors are brass 1, paired with A1's internal door 1, so "leaving" loops you into the waiting room. The lobby directory letterboard sits about 6 m up the south wall (bottom row "OUT OF OFFICE"), reached by a wall run. The cloakroom (a blank 3-slot plate) is under the L1 west gallery. Elevator doors G-4 carry numbers but aren't thresholds, a quiet red herring. The cantilevered boardroom pod at L6 is reached only by the launch.
- **A3 Open Plan (L3):**
  - Aisles of cubicles under letterboard aisle signs 31-42.
  - The enclosed records passage has a 37 plate at each end, facing inward, so it loops forever. You enter it by a side door.
  - Pull a tile from either 37 to break the loop and reach the Records Room and door 312.
  - A floor-level 412 hatch near the entry is a slide-through shortcut to the cafeteria kitchen.
- **A4 Cafeteria (L1):**
  - inside_02 wholesale: a dark hall about 50 x 50 m and 14 m tall, one skylight shaft, a hanging garden, about 120 orange shell chairs.
  - Stepped seating terraces; the balcony with door 312 is reached by a movement route.
  - The menu letterboard teaches K4.
  - The kitchen roller shutter 412 pairs with A3's hatch.
- **A5 Stair Core (L-1 to L6):** brutal_01: an open concrete stair shaft with cantilevered landings, one great curved wall and a 28 m void. At the bottom a 3 x 3 floor hatch marked 6 pairs with a high opening marked 6 on the Atrium's west wall, angled 30 degrees up. Dropping in from the top landing launches you at 26 m/s across the void onto the boardroom pod and into A6. You can't reach A6 on foot.
- **A6 Executive Wing (L6):** oxblood carpet and wood, lit glass meeting rooms in darkness (inside_01), whiteboards drawn with lines between numbers, and a director's office with an OUT OF OFFICE hang tag. The boardroom window looks out over the fog to a single free-standing lit door (the exterior threshold, K5). Behind it, readable from the window, a tall stele carries a brass **000**, and three large zeros are inlaid in the pale ground in front of it.
- **Ending:** any threshold set to 000 pairs with the exterior door. Step through onto a vast, pale, foggy plain (playdead_01). The door shuts behind you, and after 6 s the title "OOO" fades in. The player can keep walking. The pause menu still works.

## Controls, camera, scale

`CameraRig_FPS` and every kit movement ability from the first second, with no changes to `PlayerConfig`. Interact (the existing `Interact` action: E / pad West) pulls or places a letterboard tile. You carry up to three tiles, shown as small plates at the lower edge of the view (a diegetic hand, not HUD). Scale: 1 unit = 1 m, the player is 1.8 m tall, doors are 1 x 2.1 m (executive doors 1.2 x 2.4 m), ceilings are 3 m in offices and 8 m or more in halls. Keyboard and mouse, plus gamepad.

## Features with acceptance criteria

- **F1 Movement:** the kit's full move set works from the first frame. Test: the existing movement play-mode tests pass, and in A1 a fresh player can sprint, slide, jump and wall-run with no unlock.
- **F2 Threshold pairing:** crossing a paired threshold moves the player to its partner, keeping speed, turned by the frames' relative rotation. Test: frames "12" 50 m apart, B rotated 90 deg -> player crosses A at 10 m/s -> next frame the player is past B, horizontal speed is 10 +/- 0.1 m/s, velocity is A-relative direction mapped to B (+/- 1 deg), and the camera moves less than 0.05 m on screen.
- **F3 See-through:** a paired threshold renders its partner's side, and an unpaired one shows the real space. Test: pair 12/12 -> screenshot through A shows B's room. Remove one tile -> A shows its own room.
- **F4 Pairing rules:** exactly two matching numbers pair, while one, or three or more, never do. Test: three frames "5" -> cross one -> no teleport. Remove a tile from the third -> the remaining two pair.
- **F5 Movable tiles:** Interact on a letterboard tile pulls it (up to 3 carried), and Interact on an empty slot places the first carried tile. Pairing updates on the same frame. Test: plate "7" and plate "_" -> pull 7, place in "_" -> the second threshold now pairs with the other 7, and `ThresholdsRepaired` is published.
- **F6 Orientation:** floor, ceiling and wall thresholds link with velocity turned through the frame. Test: drop 20 m into floor hatch "6" -> exit from wall opening "6" angled 25 deg up -> exit speed equals entry speed (MaxSpeed-capped), pointing along the opening's normal (+/- 2 deg).
- **F7 O equals 0:** a letter O tile reads as digit 0 when matching. Test: "1O4" and "104" -> they pair.
- **F8 Last solid ground:** falling below the area kill height returns the player to the last walkable spot they stood on for 0.3 s or more, with momentum cleared and a 0.4 s fade. Test: stand on a ledge, walk off into the void -> after the fall, the player is within 0.5 m of the ledge spot with velocity 0.
- **F9 Ending:** a threshold reading 000 pairs with the exterior door, and crossing it ends the game without a game-over state. Test: set the cloakroom plate to OOO -> cross -> `EscapedBuilding` is published, the title fades in within 6-8 s, the player still moves, the pause menu opens, and there is no GameOver state.
- **F10 Expert route:** the knowledge-only route works. Test: scripted run (virtual gamepad plus checkpoints) from spawn to the exit along the expert route -> completes in under 180 s.
- **F11 Area gates:** each area is reachable only through its intended knowledge or move. Test: per area, a scripted run of the intended route reaches its entry trigger, and a teleport-free walk without it stays out (checked at the known bypass points).
- **F12 No explanation:** nothing on screen during play except the pause menu. Test: during play, every screen-space canvas group except Pause has alpha 0. Diegetic text uses world-space text only.
- **F13 Lived-in text:** every area has at least 8 diegetic text props (signage, nameplates, memos, notices, whiteboards). None gives instructions; they hint and imply. Test: a count per area is at least 8, and a text review passes against the tone rule ("an office, not a narrator").
- **F14 Flow UI:** boot shows the title and main menu, then Play, pause, resume and restart (new game) all work, with no game over. Test: the existing `GameFlow` tests pass, and the game-over panel is never shown.

## Non-goals

No enemies, combat, fail state, timer, save system (knowledge lives in the player's head), dialogue, narration, tutorial prompts, HUD, map, collectible counter, player-placed portals, room shifting (rooms never move: only links change), or new movement abilities. Audio is placeholder clips only (hum, footsteps, the threshold crossing).
