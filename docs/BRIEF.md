A first-person "metroidbrainia" about escaping an impossibly vast, empty
midcentury-modern brutalist office. Progression is gated only by knowledge:
a player who already knows everything should be able to walk out in minutes.

Nothing is ever explained directly: not the mechanics, not the world, not who
the player is or why they are here. No tutorials, dialogue, narration or HUD.
Diegetic text is welcome and encouraged (signage, room numbers, nameplates,
memos, whiteboards, notices, labels) to give the world as much detail and life
as possible, but it should feel like an office that was lived in, never like
it is speaking to the player. It can hint, imply and reward attention; it never
explains. No fail state, no timer, no game over; like BABBDI, the player can
stay as long as they like. Falling off the world returns them to the last
solid ground they stood on.

Movement: every ability from the first second (sprint, slide, chained hops,
air strafing, wall running, wall jumping, surfing) with momentum-based,
Source/Quake-style air control. Feel references: Pseudoregalia, Titanfall 2,
CS surf maps. Learning the movement is part of the knowledge: the architecture
invites each move without words, and mastering them opens new areas, shortcuts
and vantage points.

Other gates are environmental puzzles in the spirit of Myst/Riven and Outer
Wilds, solved by noticing and engaging with the place itself. The building is
quietly impossible, and that impossibility is a mechanic: its architecture
breaks the rules of real space in ways the player must notice, understand and
use. Prefer one or two impossible rules explored deeply over many shallow ones.

The fast, playful movement should sit against the slow, melancholic mood.

Scope: a vertical slice, five or more connected areas and one way out.

Mood: surreal, melancholic, foggy; cold concrete and warm wood, plastic
waiting-room chairs, cubicles, office spaces, meeting rooms, cafeterias,
philodendrons.

Art references (docs/references/), and what to take from each:
- Inside: wordless restraint, fog, scale, silhouette
- Control: brutalist interiors and lighting (do not reuse its shifting-room conceit)
- Severance: corporate emptiness, retro office furniture, unease
- Twin Peaks: warm wood against dread
- Kentucky Route Zero: theatrical staging and lighting (not its text-heavy style)

Fixed vs. open: everything above is fixed. The most open parts, and where
your invention matters most, are the puzzles themselves and the progression
through the world: what the gates are, how the areas connect, what knowledge
unlocks what, and how the player comes to understand the building's rules.
You may decide privately who the player is and why the building is like this
so the world feels coherent, but none of it is ever stated; it surfaces only
through the environment. Before committing, sketch three divergent directions
for the building's impossibility and the progression it creates, pick one and
explain why. Flag anywhere you depart from this brief.
